## Description

Invisibility Cloaking: Theory and Experiments. The Defense Intelligence Reference Document is 2 March 2010, with an ICOD of 1 December 2009 and control DIA-08-1003-001, UNCLASSIFIED//FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY. Where the Prepared by and Author appear, the document shows [(b)(3):10 USC 424] and Defense Intelligence Agency ([`/organizations/dia`]) and [(b)(6)]. An Administrative Note describes the product as one in a series of advanced technology reports produced in FY 2009 under the Defense Intelligence Agency, [(b)(3):10 USC 424] Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications (AAWSA) Program [`/organizations/aawsap`], and that comments or questions should be addressed to the AAWSA Program Manager. The document is one of a series of FY 2009 AAWSA Program advanced technology reports ([`/documents/dia-aatip-products-list-2018`]); the author ([`/people/ulf-leonhardt`]) appears there, not on the document, where the Author line shows [(b)(6)].

The paper describes the theory and experiments of invisibility cloaking. Invisibility may be achieved through three principal methods: camouflage, transparency, and cloaking. Camouflage is shown through stealth technology and optical camouflage; transparency through changing a refractive index so light is not scattered; and cloaking as the universal strategy. The paper describes cloaking as a transparent shell that guides light around the hidden object as if the light would propagate through empty space, so both the interior of the cloaking device is hidden and the act of hiding is concealed.

The paper describes the theory as a transformation of space: an optical material deforms the coordinates of space, creating the illusion that light propagates along straight lines through a virtual space while in reality it is bent around a hollow region that has become invisible. This idea of cloaking by coordinate transformations was put forward by two independent groups whose theories appeared in Science Express on 25 May 2006, and in October 2006 the first cloaking device was demonstrated for microwaves. The first prototype was designed to operate in the microwave region for a wavelength of about 3 cm, built from rings of flexible circuit board etched into split-ring resonators; a material with electromagnetic or optical properties that depends on structures much smaller than the wavelength is called a metamaterial.

The paper describes a fundamental problem of transformation-based cloaking: the demonstrated microwave-cloaking device only works correctly for a single frequency, and the speed of light must be infinitely large at the inner lining of the invisibility cloak, so cloaking of wave packets carrying information is impossible. With non-Euclidean curved-space cloaking the distortion of space is always finite rather than infinite, so such devices can in principle operate in a broad band of the spectrum. The paper describes the greatest challenge for turning invisibility from an idea into a workable device as not technology but imagination; perfect cloaking devices are impossible because they require materials where the speed of light approaches infinity, while imperfect cloaking devices implementing curved-space geometries could be made — for microwaves, definitely within reach of present technology.

### Broadband invisibility — non-Euclidean curved-space cloaking keeps the distortion of space finite rather than infinite, so the required optical properties stay finite and such devices can in principle operate in a broad band of the spectrum

> Why are such curved optical spaces of any practical advantage? They seem more complicated, but the distortion of space in such spaces is always finite, never infinite as in the conventional Euclidean cloaking devices. As the spatial distortions directly correspond to the required refractive indices, the required optical properties are never infinite, and hence such devices can, in principle, operate in a broad band of the spectrum.

### Cloaking definition — cloaking is the universal strategy in which a transparent shell guides light around the hidden object as if it propagated through empty space, hiding both the object and the act of hiding

> Cloaking⁴, ⁵ is a universal strategy for invisibility that works for objects of arbitrary compositions and shapes within a given size. In cloaking, the hidden object is enclosed by the cloaking device, a transparent shell that guides light around the object as if the light would propagate through empty space. In this way, both the interior of the cloaking device is hidden and the act of hiding is concealed.

### Microwave prototype — the first prototype cloaking device operated in the microwave region for a wavelength of about 3 cm, built from rings of flexible circuit board etched into split-ring resonators

> The first prototype¹³ of a cloaking device was designed to operate in the microwave region of the electromagnetic spectrum, for a wavelength of about 3 cm. The device consists of 10 rings of flexible circuit board. The copper of the circuit board has been etched away, apart from characteristic structures of about 3-mm size, so-called split- ring resonators.

### Transformation-optics theory — an optical material acts like a transformation of space, creating the illusion that light propagates along straight lines through a virtual space while in reality it is bent

> The material acts like a transformation of space. In the device, each point in real space is put to a new location in a virtual space (outside of the device, space remains the same). The device creates the illusion that light propagates along straight lines through the virtual space, whereas in reality it is bent.
