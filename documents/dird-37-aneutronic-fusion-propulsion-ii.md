## Description

A Defense Intelligence Reference Document in the Defense Futures series — one of a series of advanced technology reports produced in FY 2010 under the Defense Intelligence Agency ([`/organizations/dia`]), (b)(3):10 USC 424 Advanced Aerospace Weapons System Applications (AAWSA) Program ([`/organizations/aawsap`]). Where the document shows Prepared by and Authors, it shows (b)(3):10 USC 424 and (b)(6); a COPYRIGHT WARNING states that further dissemination of the photographs in this publication is not authorized, and comments or questions pertaining to the document are to be addressed to the AAWSA Program Manager, Defense Intelligence Agency, Bldg 6000, Washington D.C. The document is made available through the declassification efforts and research of John Greenewald, Jr. ([`/people/john-greenewald`]), creator of The Black Vault ([`/organizations/the-black-vault`]). It is one of the FY 2010 AAWSA Program advanced technology reports ([`/documents/dia-aatip-products-list-2018`]), and is Part II to Aneutronic Fusion Propulsion ([`/documents/dird-30-aneutronic-fusion-propulsion`]).

The report reviews the basic fusion plasma physics, design concepts that apply aneutronic fusion for propulsion, and the requirements for transitioning to space. Such aneutronic fusion fuels, such as hydrogen fusing with Boron-11, have been studied extensively for space propulsion applications since the 1980s, since non-neutron-generating fuels are adopted for mitigating shielding mass and tritium fuel launch-safety concerns over fission propulsion with its costly safety requirements. The predominant concepts studied include magnetic field reversed configuration, dense plasma focus and inertial electrostatic confinement. Controlled fusion energy production has been under development for 60 years — from Lyman Spitzer's 1951 Stellarator, through the Fusor invented by Robert Hirsch in his 1966 Ph.D. thesis (named with his thesis advisor Philo Farnsworth) and a 1983 rediscovery by Robert Bussard — and the report covers current government and privately funded programs, among them the U.S. Department of Energy Office of Fusion Energy Sciences fusion program, the international ITER undertaking, NASA's high-power electric propulsion work, and the privately funded ventures of Lawrenceville Plasma Physics (which uses dense plasma focus) and TriAlpha ([`/organizations/tri-alpha-energy`]) (which uses beam-heated field reversed configuration).

The future technology development of aneutronic fusion propulsion will initially be motivated by the very large GEO satellites that will be developed for commercial and military broadband communication. Of the concepts studied, the dense plasma focus reactor is the lightest concept at 16 metric tons and could produce 800 MW of power with 1000 kN of thrust, though the application of this fusion reactor for propelling aircraft from ground to hypersonic speeds would be very difficult. In the Conclusions, pulsed-powered DPF or IEC aneutronic fusion thrusters may have near-term applications to replace current satellite ion thrusters, which could be extended to the very high-power domain of beam-assisted FRC for manned interplanetary flight; this transition may depend upon the success of the developments of privately funded ventures attempting to develop terrestrial power; aneutronic fusion propulsion will not be practical beyond the solar system unless breakthrough propulsion physics is developed; and the future needs to be focused more on science and engineering and less on science fiction.
