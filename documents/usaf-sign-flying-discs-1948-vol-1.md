## Description

A file of Air Force correspondence on "Flying Discs," from 24 September 1947 to 15 June 1948, for Project "SIGN." Its Disposition Form, Routing and Record Sheet, letter, and CORRESPONDENCE REFERENCE FORM pages, the SECRET / CONFIDENTIAL / RESTRICTED security classification on each, the "000.9 Phenomena" file stamp, and the "DECLASSIFIED / Authority: NND 760191" stamp are set out below.


The file states the Air Force policy: it is "Air Force policy not to ignore reports of sightings and phenomena in the atmosphere, but to recognize that part of its mission is to collect, collate, evaluate, and act on information of this nature." In Implementing this policy the Air Materiel Command ([`/organizations/air-materiel-command`]) at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base was designated the Air Force agency to collect, collate, evaluate and distribute to interested government agencies and contractors all information concerning sightings and phenomena in the atmosphere which can be construed to be of concern to the national security, and was authorized direct contact with all Air Force installations to develop additional information on the flying discs. A Routing and Record Sheet states that by letter dated 30 December 1947 the Commanding General, Air Materiel Command, is instructed to "set up a project whose purpose is to collect, collate, evaluate and distribute" such information — the directive for Project SIGN. The Disposition Form is FOR THE CHIEF OF STAFF by C. P. Cabell ([`/people/c-p-cabell`]), Chief, Air Intel. Req. Div.


The file reports the Air Materiel Command proposal — Colonel McCoy ([`/people/howard-mccoy`])'s informal proposal — that fighter or night interceptor aircraft be maintained on a continuous alert status, equipped with gun camera, to secure photographs of reported unusual phenomena of the "flying disc" type, and that proposal considered unfeasible: S. E. Anderson ([`/people/s-e-anderson`]), Director of Plans and Operations, considered stationing fighter aircraft on a continuous alert status "not considered feasible" and recommended Air Materiel Command's responsibility for "collecting" information be confined to the establishment of direct channels. A 17 March 1948 letter by George C. McDonald ([`/people/george-mcdonald`]), Director of Intelligence, states proper interception is not possible, except by accident, without complete radar coverage the Air Force is incapable of providing, and that it is doubtful fighter aircraft would be able to follow up reports emanating, for the most part, from civilian sources.


Other pages concern German tailless aircraft, relating the Horten brothers to the alleged "Flying Saucer" case. A 24 September 1947 Air Materiel Command letter prepared under H. M. McCoy ([`/people/howard-mccoy`]) — "Inclosed herewith is also a report prepared by the Royal Aircraft Establishment, Technical Note, AERO 1703, which describes the Horten tailless aircraft" — and reports a U. S. Military attache, Moscow report that 1800 aircraft based on the Horten VIII design are being built for bomber squadron use; a 24 February 1948 letter states that contact has been made with the Horton Brothers. The file also forwarded sighting reports to the Chief of Staff — the Hobson, Ohio sighting received from Special Agent D.K. Brown, Federal Bureau of Investigation, Cleveland, Ohio, forwarded by H. B. Wax ([`/people/h-b-wax`]) of the Eleventh Air Force, and the Bakersfield, California observation by Mr. Les Buchner under investigation by Fourth Air Force under Donald L. Springer ([`/people/donald-springer`]). Other pages in the file are forwarded by George D. Garrett, Jr. ([`/people/george-garrett`]), Robert Taylor 3rd ([`/people/robert-taylor`]), Wilton H. Earle, Jr. ([`/people/wilton-earle`]), and Douglass W. Eiseman ([`/people/douglass-eiseman`]).
