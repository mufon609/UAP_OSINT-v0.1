## Description

An Introduction to the Statistical Drake Equation. This Defense Intelligence Reference Document, 11 March 2010, with an ICOD of 1 December 2009, UNCLASSIFIED//FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY, is one in a series of advanced technology reports produced in FY 2009 under the Defense Intelligence Agency ([`/organizations/dia`]) Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications (AAWSA) Program ([`/organizations/aawsap`]). The Prepared by is given as the Defense Intelligence Agency, with the (b)(3) part and the (b)(6) Author both marked on the cover; the author name is given on the reproduced Appendix B paper as Claudio Maccone ([`/people/claudio-maccone`]), and the report is attributed off the document on another Defense Intelligence document ([`/documents/dia-aatip-products-list-2018`]). This document is made available through the declassification efforts and research of John Greenewald, Jr. ([`/people/john-greenewald`]), creator of The Black Vault.


The report takes the Drake equation — the 1961 product of seven factors yielding N, the number of communicating extraterrestrial civilizations now in the galaxy — and proposed to replace each of the seven independent variables by a mean value plus or minus a certain standard deviation, so that each factor becomes a random variable rather than a single number. To choose the probability distribution of each factor, the solution comes from Shannon ([`/people/claude-shannon`])'s Information Theory and a 1948 theorem that the distribution having maximum entropy over any finite range of real values is the uniform distribution over that range, so each Drake factor is a uniform random variable. Applying the Central Limit Theorem of statistics to the natural log of the product of the seven factors, the natural log of N tends to a Gaussian and thus N tends to the lognormal distribution.


For the example the statistical mean of N comes out at about 4590 communicating civilizations now in the galaxy, higher than the 3500 provided by the classical Drake equation, which the author reads as increasing the hope of finding an extraterrestrial civilization. Assuming the ET civilizations are uniformly distributed throughout the galaxy volume, the report works out the probability distribution of the distance to the nearest ET civilization — a density Paul Davies ([`/people/paul-davies`]) dubbed the "Maccone distribution" — and finds that, with 75 percent probability, the nearest extraterrestrial civilization is located between the distances of 1361 and 3979 light years from us under the example inputs. The Conclusions present a "Data Enrichment Principle": in the long run the Statistical Drake equation might become a huge computer code growing in size and in the depth of the scientific information it contains, so that it would be Humanity's first "Encyclopaedia Galactica." Appendix B reproduced in full the author's original 2008 IAC paper #IAC-08-A4.1.4, "The Statistical Drake Equation," with the name of Claudio Maccone, Co-Vice Chair of the SETI Permanent Study Group of the International Academy of Astronautics.
