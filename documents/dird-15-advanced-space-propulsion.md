## Description

Advanced Space Propulsion Based on Vacuum (Spacetime Metric) Engineering. The author is H.E. Puthoff, Ph.D. ([`/people/hal-puthoff`]) of EarthTech International, Inc., Austin, Texas; the document was prepared by the Acquisition Support Division (DWO-3) of the Defense Warning Office, Directorate for Analysis, Defense Intelligence Agency ([`/organizations/aaro`]), 29 March 2010, with an ICOD of 1 December 2009. An Administrative Note states the product is one in a series of advanced technology reports produced in FY 2009 under the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications (AAWSA) Program and is addressed to James T. Lacatski, D.Eng. ([`/people/james-lacatski`]), AAWSA Program Manager. UNCLASSIFIED//FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY appears on every page.

The paper considered the concept that empty space itself — the quantum vacuum, or spacetime metric — might be engineered to provide energy/thrust for future space vehicles. Puthoff states the proposal is solidly grounded in modern physical theory: "engineering the vacuum" was introduced by Nobelist T. D. Lee, and a statement acknowledging the central role of the vacuum in modern physics is provided by 2004 Nobelist Frank Wilczek. The vacuum is characterized as an energetic and structured medium: within quantum theory the seat of energetic particle and field fluctuations, and within general relativity the seat of a spacetime structure (metric) that encodes the distribution of matter and energy.

The analysis is a model-independent, "general relativity for engineers" approach: the metric tensor that describes the measurement of spacetime intervals is used, and it assumes only that a technology exists that can control and manipulate (engineer) the spacetime metric. Beginning by cataloging associated physical effects from alterations in the metric tensor coefficients — alteration of time interval, frequency, energy, spatial interval, velocity of light, effective mass, and gravity/antigravity "forces" — the effects are summarized in Table 1 as interpreted by a remote observer. It then examines the significance of these effects for advanced aerospace craft technologies — time alteration, blueshift of a craft's emissions, refractive index effects such as light-bending and apparent "cloaking", reduction of effective mass, and gravity/antigravity/propulsion effects. As one exemplar, Alcubierre's warp drive is discussed, a spacetime metric solution of Einstein's field equation that would allow superluminal travel as observed by an exterior observer without violating the local velocity-of-light constraint, and which requires one to construct a warp bubble of exotic matter with negative energy. The Discussion considered the right-hand column of Table 1 to presumably describe an ideal craft for interstellar travel, with such features in conformance with general relativity, while the required energy densities exceed by many orders of magnitude values achievable with existing engineering techniques.

### Thesis statement — empty space might be engineered to provide energy/thrust for future space vehicles

> A theme that has come to the fore in advanced planning for long-range space exploration in the future is the concept that empty space itself (the quantum vacuum, or spacetime metric) might be engineered to provide energy/thrust for future space vehicles. Although far reaching, such a proposal is solidly grounded in modern physical theory, and therefore the possibility that matter/vacuum interactions might be engineered for spaceflight applications is not a priori ruled out (Reference 1).

### Superluminal-transport mechanism — engineered metric permits apparent faster-than-light travel to an external observer without locally violating the velocity-of-light constraint

> Given that velocities in general in different coordinate systems scale as does the velocity of light—that is, v → √(g_00/-g_11) v —for exotic propulsion an engineered spacetime metric can in principle establish a condition in which the trajectory of a craft approaching the velocity of light in its own frame would be observed from an exterior frame to exceed light speed—that is, exhibit motion at superluminal speed. This opens up the possibility of transport at superluminal velocities (as measured by an external observer) without violation of the velocity-of-light constraint within the spacetime-altered region, a feature attractive for interstellar travel.

### Alcubierre warp drive exemplar — expansion of space behind and contraction in front of the craft, "surfing on a wave" of spacetime geometry

> As one exemplar, consider Alcubierre's formulation of a "warp drive," a spacetime metric solution of Einstein's GR field equation (References 2, 22). Alcubierre derived a spacetime metric motivated by cosmological inflation that would allow arbitrarily short travel times between two distant points in space. The behavior of the warp drive metric provides for the simultaneous expansion of space behind the spacecraft and a corresponding contraction of space in front of the spacecraft (see Figure 3). The warp drive spacecraft would thus appear to be "surfing on a wave" of spacetime geometry.

### Warp-bubble requirement — a shell of exotic matter with negative energy and/or negative pressure, unlikely to be met in the foreseeable future

> In order to implement a warp drive, one would have to construct a "warp bubble" that surrounded the spacecraft by generating a thin shell or surface layer of exotic matter—that is, a quantum field having negative energy and/or negative pressure. Although the technical requirements for such are unlikely to be met in the foreseeable future (Reference 22), the exercise nonetheless serves as a good example for showcasing attributes associated with manipulation of the spacetime metric at will.
