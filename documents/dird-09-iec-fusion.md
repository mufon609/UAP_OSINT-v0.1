## Description

Inertial Electrostatic Confinement Fusion. The Defense Intelligence Reference Document is 10 March 2010, with an ICOD of 1 December 2009 and control DIA-08-1003-006, UNCLASSIFIED//FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY. Where the Prepared by and Author appear, the document shows [(b)(3):10 USC 424] and Defense Intelligence Agency ([`/organizations/dia`]) and [(b)(6)]. An Administrative Note names the product as one in a series of advanced technology reports produced in FY 2009 under the Defense Intelligence Agency, [(b)(3):10 USC 424] Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications (AAWSA) Program [`/organizations/aawsap`], and that comments or questions should be addressed to the AAWSA Program Manager. The document is one of a series of FY 2009 AAWSA Program advanced technology reports ([`/documents/dia-aatip-products-list-2018`]); the author ([`/people/george-miley`]) appears there, not on the document, where the Author line shows [(b)(6)].

The report provides an overview of the basics, current experimental status, supporting theory, and potential applications of inertial electrostatic confinement (IEC) fusion, with emphasis on work at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. IEC was conceived of by Philo Farnsworth ([`/people/philo-farnsworth`]) as an approach to fusion power using electrostatic fields for confinement. In the gridded IEC device a spherical mesh grid is suspended on a high voltage feed-through in the center of a metal vacuum vessel; the fuel gas, e.g. deuterium, is fed into the chamber and the high negative voltage on the grid extracts ions from the plasma and accelerates them toward the center, where the ions are trapped in a potential well and recirculate many times, giving a finite probability of fusing. For breakeven, J. Lawson ([`/people/john-lawson`]) developed his "criterion" nτ = 10¹⁴ cm⁻³-sec at T > 15 keV for DT fusion. The report shows that IEC is one of the few potential fusion approaches that can potentially burn aneutronic fuels like p-¹¹B (hydrogen – Boron 11), in which neutrons carry no more than 1% of the total released energy.

The report reviewed select experiments — the early Hirsch ([`/people/robert-hirsch`]) ion-injected IEC that exceeded 10⁹ DT n/s at 150 kV, the STAR mode discharge, and IEC neutron source work licensed by Daimler-Chrysler — and other geometries, including cylindrical IECs, an electrically-driven IEC jet thruster intended as an ultra-maneuverable space thruster, the Dipole Assisted IEC (DaIEC), and Khachan's ([`/people/joe-khachan`]) studies at the University of Sydney. A theory section compares the pessimistic Q ~ 0.21 result of Nevins ([`/people/william-nevins`]) for a 50-kV square well against the bounce-averaged Fokker-Planck (BAFP) results of Chacon ([`/people/luis-chacon`]), the deep double well study of Tzonev ([`/people/ivan-tzonev`]) et al., the stability "window" found by Kim ([`/people/h-j-kim`]), and the Bremsstrahlung loss and energy balance critique of Rider ([`/people/todd-rider`]).

The report provides an overview of potential applications — neutron/proton/x-ray sources, an integrated x-ray/neutron inspection system for explosives and nuclear material detection, and space propulsion, including the "Fusion Ship II" 750-MWe IEC fusion-powered manned spacecraft and the Magnetically-Channeled Spherical IEC Array (MCSA) concept. Present experimental devices are four to five orders of magnitude below breakeven energy gain for p-¹¹B; the report concludes with a conceptual proposal for a 12 gun breakeven experiment that would confirm break-even conditions using a hydrogen plasma simulation of p-¹¹B, and argued there are no "show stoppers" to the road map to IEC fusion power.
