## Description

Anomalous Acute and Subacute Field Effects on Human Biological Tissues. The Defense Intelligence Reference Document is 11 March 2010, with an ICOD of 1 December 2009; UNCLASSIFIED//FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY is struck through with a black strike line on every page, and the cover shows the Defense Intelligence Reference Document banner and the Acquisition Threat Support series. On the page where the Prepared by and Author appear, the document shows [box: (b)(3):10 USC 424] and Defense Intelligence Agency, and the Author line shows [box: (b)(6) — author name redacted], so no author name appears on the document. An Administrative Note states the product is one in a series of advanced technology reports produced in FY 2009 under the Defense Intelligence Agency, [(b)(3):10 USC 424] Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications (AAWSA) Program, and that comments or questions should be addressed to the AAWSA Program Manager. The document is one of a series of FY 2009 AAWSA Program advanced technology reports ([`/documents/dia-aatip-products-list-2018`]); the author ([`/people/kit-green`]) appears there, not on the document, whose author line is redacted.

The Preface states that three previous fit and active individuals experienced an anomalous aerospace-related event and within 72 hours suffered medical signs and symptoms — almost immediate erythema over exposed skin, fever, pain, headaches, numbness and parasthesiae, cardiac palpitations, photophobia, and in one case moderate blood dyscrasia and signs of radiation illness; the three were antennae engineers subjected to an accidental exposure in the near-field to a broad-band ultrahigh radiofrequency mixed radiation of RF, NIEMR, and microwave energies centered at about 785 MHz. An extensive review of the medical literature revealed an additional 42 cases from the peer-reviewed literature and an additional un-published similar 300 cases of mixed exposures of from 1 –10 GHz at power densities of above 100 mW/cm². The Summary states the paper relates, summarizes, and analyzes evidence of unintended injury to human observers by anomalous advanced aerospace systems, and that the work can inform — e.g., reverse engineer — through clinical diagnoses certain physical characteristics of possible future advanced aerospace systems from unknown provenance. Sufficient incidents and medical data support a hypothesis that some advanced systems are already deployed, and opaque to full US understanding, and the medical analyses indicate the use of (to us) unconventional and advanced energy systems.

The most important bandwidth of interest is from about 300 kHz to 300 GHz, and the document describes the biophysics of how damage occurs: the term radiofrequency covers 100 kHz-300 GHz and the term microwave 300 MHz-300 GHz; tissues such as blood, skin, brain, and peripheral nerves will absorb much more energy than fat and bone; the threshold of heat perception is near 0.7 mW/cm², with power densities of about 9.0 mW/cm² causing sensations of pain within one second. The use of very high intensity RF pulses at 915 MHz will cause an elevation in brain temperature of 8 °C, resulting in petit mal or grand mal seizures and unconsciousness; the threshold for this stun effect is 680 J, and no adverse effects have been found at SARs in the head of 0.2 up to 1.0 W/kg. In the cases this reviewer has studied, the injury severity and patterns are highly consistent between the three antenna engineers and the Cash-Landrum cases. The purpose of the paper is to argue that data exists to reverse engineer propulsion systems of anomalous aerospace vehicles, independent of origin, based on biology. Appendix A presents the Schuessler catalog, UFO-Related Human Physiological Effects, a compilation by MUFON's past Director, John F. Schuessler, a summary of 356 selected cases of UFO-induced physiological effects on humans during close encounters over the time period 1873 – 1994.
