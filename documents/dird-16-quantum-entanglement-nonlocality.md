## Description

The Space-Communication Implications of Quantum Entanglement and Nonlocality reviews quantum entanglement and nonlocality and considers the possibility that this phenomenon could be used for sending observer-to-observer signals. It is one in a series of advanced technology reports produced in FY 2009 under the Defense Intelligence Agency ([`/organizations/dia`]), Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications (AAWSA) Program ([`/organizations/aawsap`]); the author ([`/people/john-cramer`]) appears in the program's series of these reports ([`/documents/dia-aatip-products-list-2018`]), while no author appears on the document itself.

The paper's thesis is that such a demonstration would break several quantum "no-signal theorems" in the physics literature, and that nonlocal quantum signaling would have far-reaching implications as an enabling technology for superluminal and retrocausal signaling. The Foreword and Introduction observe that communication in space at the scale of the solar system is severely limited by the speed of light, and that this time delay makes real-time control of remote space-based devices impossible. The paper holds that relativity prohibits only certain forms of superluminal communication, while quantum nonlocality — the quantum connectedness acting in entangled quantum systems — appears, at least superficially, to exhibit superluminal aspects while preserving compatibility with special relativity. The question investigated is whether quantum nonlocality is the private domain of nature or whether it can be used to send signals from one observer to another; the paper states there is at present no compelling answer.

Section II reviews the no-signal theorems: a number of authors have presented "proofs" that nonlocal observer-to-observer communication is impossible within the formalism of standard quantum mechanics, but the paper points out a recent observation that at least some of these "proofs" are tautological, assuming the measurement process and its associated Hamiltonian are local and thereby building the no signaling conclusion into their starting assumptions. Section III holds there is no conflict between nonlocal signaling and the Lorentz invariance of special relativity, while the principle of causality appears very likely to be violated (or at least violate-able) if nonlocal signaling is possible.

Section IV turns to the momentum domain of Einstein's ([`/people/albert-einstein`]) original focus, where it appears nonlocal quantum communication may be more easily achieved. The Ghost Interference experiment of the Shih ([`/people/yanhua-shih`]) group demonstrates the nonlocal connection between entangled photons, but in the form reported does not demonstrate nonlocal communication because of its use of a classical communication link to impose the coincidence requirement between the detected photons. The paper describes a slit-imaging coincidence-free version: if the pattern observed by the camera can be changed by switching the optical fiber routing, this would constitute a direct demonstration of nonlocal communication and would falsify the no-signal theorems. Later in the paper, the applications are considered — by the introduction of delay paths the "send" instant could be made to occur well after the "receive" instant, constituting a direct demonstration of retrocausal signaling and a direct violation of the principle of causality, and a "rover" operated at interplanetary distances, where the nonlocal control signal sent backwards up the time stream would arrive at the probe at the instant its video and data were transmitted, so that an Earth-based operator could steer and control the probe on Mars in real time using virtual-reality techniques.

Section XII describes another path, nonlinear quantum mechanics: the no-signal "proofs" become invalid if quantum mechanics is allowed to be slightly nonlinear, a technical term for a small cross-term not present in the standard formalism. Two years after Steven Weinberg's ([`/people/steven-weinberg`]) nonlinear quantum mechanics theory was published, Joseph Polchinski ([`/people/joseph-polchinski`]) showed that the nonlinear corrections upset the balance preventing superluminal communication using EPR experiments. Earth-based experimental tests for the nonlinear effects have produced only very low upper limits, but work on quantum gravity offers a curved-space wave equation in which strong space curvature — for example, the vicinity of a neutron star or black hole — means sufficient quantum nonlinearity may exist to facilitate nonlocal communication. The Conclusion states that ultimately the question of whether nonlocal communication is possible is an experimental one, resolvable by testing for nonlocal communication and observing what experimental limits appear.
